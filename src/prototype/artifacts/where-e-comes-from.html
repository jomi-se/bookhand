Chapter XIV · True compound interest

Where e comes from

£100 at 100% a year. Add the interest in more and more often, and the money doesn't explode. It creeps up on a strange number.

interest added
…and if continued for ten years (which will multiply by this factor ten times over) will multiply the original capital by 2.59374. Every extra split makes each bit of interest smaller as well as more frequent. The two effects nearly cancel. What's left over is e = \lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\tfrac1n\right)^n, the growth of a thing whose growth is always proportional to itself.
