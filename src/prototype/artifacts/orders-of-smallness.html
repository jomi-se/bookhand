Chapter II · Degrees of smallness

Minùtes of minùtes

Why is a second called a second? Because it is the second order of smallness: a small part of a small part.

…they divided each minute into 60 still smaller parts, which, in Queen Elizabeth's days, they called "second minùtes."

Small is a comparison

A farthing is small next to a sovereign. Next to a thousand pounds it is second-order small. Tap through.

A farthing and a sovereign

About 1⁄1000 of it. Small, but you can see it.

A farthing and £1000

A farthing is to £1000 what 1⁄1000 of a farthing is to a sovereign. It's gone.

where did it go?!

…but multiply it by a few hundred

500 farthings is about half a sovereign. A small thing times a big thing can matter again.

 Thompson's warning: small quantities multiplied by something large stop being negligible. Keep that in mind for Keep or neglect?
