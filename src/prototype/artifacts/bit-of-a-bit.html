The bit of a bit

A square of side x grows by a sliver dx. Drag the sliver and watch which new pieces matter.

Let dx be of x.

Two strips, 2·x·dx
The corner, (dx)²

(x + dx)^2 = x^2 + 2x\,dx + (dx)^2
The second term is not negligible because it is a first-order quantity; while the third term is of the second order of smallness, being a bit of, a bit of x². ↑ he means the little blue corner! One ten-thousandth. A bit (1⁄100) of a bit (1⁄100). The two strips together are still 2⁄100: two hundred times bigger than the corner. Thompson's rule of thumb: make dx small enough and you keep the first-order bits and throw away the bit of a bit. That throwing-away is the whole trick of differentiation.
